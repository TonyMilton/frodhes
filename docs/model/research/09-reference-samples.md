# Reference recordings and published measurements for calibration

Research agent 9, 2026-10-15. The goal is to find recordings of real Rhodes pianos (Mark I first, Mark II/Suitcase second) that we can **analyse**: two-stage decay per key, partial ratios, velocity → timbre ("bark"), release/damper behaviour, attack pitch and noise floor. Only derived numbers are committed, never audio. The goal is also to collect published measurements we can use directly.

Provenance tags follow note 05. **DERIVED** means computed by me from cited data with a stated method. **ESTIMATE** means my judgement. Paths starting `sources/` are relative to `docs/model/research/sources/09-reference-samples/`. No audio was downloaded. For ETI ROADS I read only zip directory metadata and the first 1 KB of 242 entries (the NCW headers, which give the sample count) through HTTP Range requests, just as note 05 read FLAC STREAMINFO.

## Summary

**Top 3 recommendations**

1. **ETI ROADS (Rhodes Mark II, 1980, free)** is the most complete dataset found by a wide margin, and the only one documented like a research dataset, by a 67-page bachelor thesis (`sources/eti-roads-thesis-telik-2023.txt`). It has **all 73 keys** at full length, **DI only** (RME Fireface 800 instrument input, no amp or mic), **96 kHz/24-bit mono**, and **42 velocity layers per key** spaced about 1 dB in peak level from about −13 to −76 dBFS. It also has **37 release samples per key**, taken at different tine amplitudes, plus **11 pedal-down and 11 pedal-up samples** and **70 s of raw instrument noise**. The peak level of every file is encoded in its file name. Caveats: it is a Mark II; the samples are denoised (iZotope RX9 spectral de-noise plus a steep 35 Hz high-pass); the files are Kontakt NCW; and the EULA forbids "breaking down" the product. Analyse it by rendering through full Kontakt, or ask the author first (see Licence notes). Score 5/5 for coverage, 4/5 overall.
2. **jRhodes3d (Rhodes Mark I Stage 73, 1977, free)** is still the cleanest **Mark I** set with a usable licence: the samples are CC BY-NC, and the measurements we derive are facts. It is DI, unlooped and full length, but it was **EQ'd at recording and denoised**, and it has only 15 roots × 3–5 layers and no release or noise samples. Use it for Mark I-specific spectra, partial ratios, attack pitch and the shape of bark versus layer. Do not use its spectral tilt or noise floor at face value. Score 4/5.
3. **Pianobook "Matt's Fender Rhodes" (Mark I 73, DI via Countryman Type 10 into a Chandler TG2, "no processing", free)** is the only **unprocessed Mark I DI** set found, so it is the reference for the true noise floor, the true spectral tilt and late decay. Its sample counts are unpublished, so they must be inspected after download. **Licence risk:** the Pianobook EULA limits use to "your own newly-created sound recording(s) and/or performances". It also says Products "may not be used in or in relation to any competitive products that are sold". Get written permission from the samplist (Matt Blostein) before committing numbers derived from it. Score 4/5 technically, 2/5 on licence.

**Runner-up with the cleanest licence:** Freesound pack 3957 by tim.kahn. It is a **Mark II 73 recorded "directly from the harp into a Bellari tube preamp"**, with 42 single-velocity notes, mono AIFF, under **CC BY 4.0**. It is good for unprocessed decay, partial ratios and noise floor, and it can be cited and even redistributed with attribution. Score 3/5.

**Already on disk:** the UVI A4 recordings at 8 hammer forces that ship with Falaize's code (`sources/03-falaize-phs/code/.../REF/REF1..8.wav`, 48 kHz/16-bit, 5 s each). They give an immediate velocity → timbre check on one note. Their licence is unknown, so use them for analysis only.

**Most useful published measurements** (details are under Published measurements found):
- **Measured tine Q** (Shear 2011, 1974 Mark I 88): Q = 731 to 2175. **DERIVED** T60 ranges from 26.8 s at D#2 down to 3.1 s at D#6. Five midrange keys (B3–D#4) measure Q 1040–1520, i.e. T60 8.3–10.7 s.
- **Tone-bar and tine frequencies** (Muenster & Pfeifle, ISMA 2014): the tone bar's own f0 is far below the tine's, e.g. C4 tine 263 Hz against tone bar 105 Hz. The tone bar is driven at the tine frequency, in phase or anti-phase depending on the key. **Hammer contact time averages 6.42 ms.** The waveform settles 10–14 ms after the strike.
- **Tine overtone ratios 7.11 and 20.25** (Gabrielli et al. 2020 SLDV, as quoted by Sønderbo). The JASA paper itself is paywalled; that is a gap.
- **Per-key tine lengths, 82 tines** (**DERIVED** from the vector drawing of the Vintage Vibe cutting chart): 156.7 mm at E1 down to 23.4 mm at E7. This agrees with Sønderbo's service-manual list (156.4 mm to 22.6 mm).
- **Service-manual geometry**: hammer-tip durometer and height per key range; escapement per register; strike line 2-1/4" (bass) to 1/8" (treble); tine-to-pickup gap 1/16"–1/8" (0.020" possible after 1972); damper clearance 3/8"–1/2"; harp output impedance about 2500 Ω.
- **DI dynamic range per key** (**DERIVED**, ETI file names): about **56 dB** of peak level on average (47–63 dB) between the softest and loudest playable strike.

## Licence notes

These are a researcher's reading of the terms, not legal advice. In general, a measured number (a T60, a ratio, a level) is a fact about the instrument and not a copy of the recording. The exception is a licence that restricts the *use* of the product as a contract, which several here do. Our rule stays as before: audio lives only in `.local/reference/`, and only numbers are committed.

- **jRhodes3d / 3c.** The `LICENSE` says: "This sample set is provided free for use as a musical instrument." It continues: "To distribute the samples themselves … the jRhodes samples are licensed under CC BY-NC 4.0." CC licences do not restrict analysis, and measurements are not adaptations. **Allowed:** analysis and committing derived numbers, crediting Jeff Learman. **Forbidden:** redistributing the audio in a commercial product. The author offers commercial licences (jjlearman@gmail.com), so ask if we ever want to ship audio.
- **ETI ROADS** (`sources/eti-roads-site-and-eula.txt`). §2.1: "the licensee is not entitled to break down the product or its parts, to have it broken down, to technically trace it back". §3: "The use of this product … to create a sound library or as a sound library for any type of synthesizer, virtual instrument, sample library, sample-based product or other musical instrument is strictly forbidden." The developer (Eduard Telik) also said on a forum, quoted second-hand, that the samples cannot be used commercially because the recording gear was the university's. **Reading:** fitting a physical model's parameters does not put samples into an instrument, but decoding the NCW files could count as "breaking down". **Do:** render notes through full Kontakt, which is ordinary use, or better, email the author for written permission to analyse and publish derived numbers. The site contact (info@eti-roads.com) is dead with the domain, so reach him through the thesis or HfM Detmold ETI. **Forbidden:** redistributing the audio, and using it in any instrument.
- **Pianobook packs** (Matt's, Wonder, mRhodes; `sources/pianobook-terms-conditions.txt`). §5: "use the download Sound File(s) only within your own newly-created sound recording(s) and/or performances". Also: "licensed to you only for use as part of a live or recorded musical performance". And: "Products may not be used in or in relation to any competitive products that are sold or relicensed to any third parties". §3 forbids "data mining tools". **Reading:** analysis for a commercial EP firmware falls **outside** the licence wording. **Do:** ask the samplist (Matt Blostein, blostein.com) and Pianobook for written permission before committing any derived number. Until then, use the audio for listening comparison only.
- **Freesound tim.kahn pack.** **CC BY 4.0**: analysis is allowed, and even redistribution, with attribution ("tim.kahn, freesound.org pack 3957"). This is the cleanest licence found.
- **IRCAM/UVI A4 references.** No licence is stated for the WAVs (note 03). Use them locally for analysis only and never redistribute them.
- **MPC-Samples Electric Keys 2** (`sources/mpcsamples-electric-keys-2-user-guide.txt`, p.42). The licence is "to use … to create Musical Compositions". It continues: "Under no circumstances can The Product, or modified versions of The Product, be re-distributed, re-sold, sub-licensed, or used to form any part of a commercial sample library." Analysis is not addressed; the publisher invites questions to licensing@mpc-samples.com. It is moot anyway, because the samples are looped.
- **Fracture Sounds.** Its EULA forbids "Use our products to train AI models, machine learning systems, or similar technologies … including using our products to generate sounds for training purposes." Fitting a deterministic physical model is not ML training, but "similar technologies" is vague. **Do not use it** without asking.
- **NI (Scarbee, Kontakt Factory), Soniccouture, Orchestral Tools, Spitfire, UVI, Adam Monroe.** These are proprietary, encrypted or plugin-only, and I did not read their EULAs. They typically allow only music production. Render-and-measure is possible only with what the user already owns, and any number published from them should be treated as needing permission.
- **Service manual, papers, theses.** Facts and numbers may be cited. The text copies in `sources/09-reference-samples/` are local reference only and should be added to the "Not committed" list in `SOURCES.md` (not edited by me).

Analysis method, the same for all sources:
- **Pitch**: YIN or a phase-vocoder f0 in a 20 ms hop for the first 500 ms, then 100 ms windows. Report Δcents(t) against the steady state.
- **Partials**: peak-pick a high-resolution STFT on 5–60 ms. Report f_k/f0, and the per-partial decay rate from a linear fit to the dB track.
- **Two-stage decay**: band-pass at f0 (±1/6 octave), Hilbert envelope in dB, piecewise-linear fit with two segments. Report τ1, τ2, the knee time and the knee level, and T60 from each stage.
- **Bark**: H2/H1, (H2+H4)/(H1+H3) and the spectral centroid of the steady 50–300 ms window, against peak dBFS.
- **Release**: time for the f0 band to fall 40 dB after note-off, against the level at note-off. Also the energy of any thump in the first 30 ms.
- **Noise floor**: Welch PSD of the silent segments in dBFS/Hz, and the hum line levels.

## Capture spec for our own recordings

Use this for a real Rhodes, or for a Nord as a comparison.

**Instrument state**
- Log the model, year (from the serial and date stamps), harp type and hammer tips.
- Tune to A440 and log the actual A4 pitch in cents.
- Do not re-voice the piano. Photograph the tine and pickup positions on E1, C3, C4, C5 and C6, with a ruler in shot.
- Stage piano: volume at max, bass boost off. Suitcase: record the harp line before the Peterson preamp if possible; otherwise preamp flat with vibrato off.
- Room temperature: log it.

**Signal chain**
- Harp output → short cable → Hi-Z input of at least 1 MΩ (an interface instrument input or a passive DI into a clean preamp). No EQ, compression or denoise.
- 96 kHz/24-bit mono.
- Gain fixed for the whole session, so that the loudest bass strike peaks at about −6 dBFS. Log the gain.
- Optionally run a second channel: a contact mic or piezo on the tone bar (to measure contact time and tone-bar motion), or a phone at 240 fps for hammer contact.

**Notes**
- All 73 keys if time allows.
- Minimum set (every minor third plus the extremes): E1 G1 A#1 C#2 E2 G2 A#2 C#3 E3 G3 A#3 C#4 E4 G4 A#4 C#5 E5 G5 A#5 C#6 E6 G6 A#6 C#7 E7 (25 keys). Add any odd key, such as a dead tine.

**Velocities**
- Play each key repeatedly from softest to hardest. Afterwards, sort the takes by measured peak level, as ETI did (target spacing about 3 dB, giving 12–18 layers from about −70 to −6 dBFS).
- Keep at least 3 takes at about −40 dBFS and 3 at about −12 dBFS, to estimate round-robin spread.
- If a MIDI-to-solenoid or mechanical striker is available, use fixed velocities instead.

**Hold and release**
- *Sustain takes*: hold the key until the note reaches the noise floor, or for 60 s (E1–B2), 40 s (C3–B4), 25 s (C5–B5) or 15 s (C6–E7), whichever comes first.
- *Release takes*: at peak levels of about −12, −24 and −40 dBFS, release after 0.3, 1, 3 and 8 s. Keep 2 s after the release.
- *Silent key*: press and release slowly, with no strike, ×3 per register, to get damper and key noise alone.
- *Pedal*: down hard, down soft, up hard and up soft, ×5 each, with all notes silent. Then hold the pedal down, strike C3, and release the pedal at 2 s, to get pedal-up damping.
- *Noise floor*: 60 s with nothing touched at volume max, 60 s at volume minimum, and 10 s with the amp or interface input unplugged (reference floor).

**File naming**

The pattern is `<src>_<midi:03d>_<note>_<type>_p<peakdBFS×10:03d>_r<release_s×10:03d>_t<take:02d>.wav`, where:
- type is `sus`, `rel`, `key` (silent key), `pdn` or `pup` (pedal), or `nse` (noise);
- peak is unsigned, so `p156` means −15.6 dBFS;
- r is `000` for sustain takes.

Example: `mk1-own_045_A2_rel_p240_r010_t02.wav`.

Keep a `takes.csv` sidecar with: file, midi, type, peak_dbfs, release_s, take, gain_setting, timestamp, notes.

**Nord or other digital sources** (comparison only, never ground truth)
- Same chain and naming, with `src = nord-<model>-<sample name>`.
- Fix the MIDI velocities at 1, 16, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 112 and 127, and give note-offs at fixed times.
- Note that Nord samples are looped or truncated and proprietary. Use them to check perceptual targets, not decay physics.

## Gaps

- **Gabrielli et al. 2020 (JASA)**: the per-key distribution of tine modes across the keyboard (SLDV) is paywalled; there is no preprint on arXiv, IRIS or ResearchGate. Only the ratios 7.11/20.25 are known, second-hand. **Action:** get it through a library, or email the corresponding author (Leonardo Gabrielli, Università Politecnica delle Marche).
- **Pfeifle & Münster 2017** (Springer chapter "Tone Production of the Wurlitzer and Rhodes E-Pianos") and the DAGA 2017 short paper: the DAGA paper has no numbers; the chapter was not accessed.
- **Wendland 2009** (TU Berlin thesis cited by DAFx-17): not found online.
- **There is still no full-keyboard measured T60 or two-stage decay table for a Mark I.** The Shear Q values cover 5 + 5 keys of an 88. ETI gives full-keyboard lengths for a Mark II, not T60s. The acquisition plan above fills this.
- **Release and damper decay times, vertical tine offset in mm, pickup coil R/L, and Peterson vibrato rate/depth**: no published values were found.
- **ETI ROADS** is a Mark II. The tone generator is generally regarded as the same as the Mark I's, but that is an unverified **ESTIMATE**. Its denoise and 35 Hz HPF bias the floor and the very-low-level tail. The raw `NOISE` file partly compensates.
- **Matt's Fender Rhodes and Wonder Rhodes**: layer and sample counts, and Wonder's chain, are unpublished until downloaded. Their licences need explicit permission.
- **Orchestral Tools Roads, Spitfire LABS and Scarbee**: chain details and EULAs were not read, because they are proprietary and low priority.
- The ETI EULA and the developer's "no commercial use" remark conflict with any commercial firmware use of derived data until permission is obtained.
