**Key warning:** this paper is qualitative. It gives the model structure and equations, but **no numeric physical parameters** (no E, ρ, tine dimensions, hammer k/α/λ, grid size, time step, pickup geometry or per-key data). The only numbers are measurement setup details, the Wurlitzer plate voltage, FPGA operator counts (Table 1) and axis ranges you can read off the figures. Every other numeric value in this document is labelled ESTIMATE or comes from another cited source.

## Summary

- The paper presents FPGA (Xilinx Virtex-7 690T) real-time finite-difference (FD) physical models of the Rhodes and of the Wurlitzer EP200. It extends earlier simpler models: Pfeifle & Muenster 2017 [18], where the tine was a simple harmonic oscillator, and Muenster & Pfeifle 2014 [17], [19].
- **Rhodes:** a hysteretic Hunt–Crossley hammer (Eq. 1) excites a cantilever **shear beam** in **two transverse polarisations** (vertical V and horizontal H). The beam has non-homogeneous mass, with the tuning spring treated as extra mass. A Kirchhoff-like tension term (Eqs. 2–3) couples the polarisations and adds large-deflection nonlinearity.
- **Rhodes pickup:** the tine tip is a point moving on a circular arc (radius r) in front of an idealised pickup tip. A magnetic-monopole/charge model gives the field B_z(x', z') as a 3-segment integral over the pole-piece profile (Eqs. 6–8). The output is the EMF, the time derivative of the flux (Eq. 4). The field is **precomputed** ("pre-processed approximation").
- The nonlinear pickup weighting gives the Rhodes waveform and "bark". Shifting the tine's rest position relative to the magnet centre (Fig. 7b) produces the asymmetric, harmonic-rich output.
- **Wurlitzer:** a 1-polarisation non-uniform beam plus a time-varying-capacitor pickup (Eqs. 9–12).
- **Time integration:** symplectic Euler (Eq. 16). Spatial FD operators are written as fixed-point register/shift/mult/add primitives (Eqs. 13–15, Table 1).
- **Validation:** high-speed camera tracking of tine/reed motion and direct-out voltages (Figs. 3–5). The models are compared only qualitatively (Fig. 11 vs Fig. 4).
- There is no amp, tremolo, cabinet or mechanical-noise model.

Signal flow (Rhodes):

```
hammer v0 --(Hunt-Crossley F, Eq.1)--> 2-pol shear beam FD (Eq.2/16) --tip (uV,uH)--> circular-arc map (Eq.8) --> precomputed B(x',y') field (Eq.7) --> d/dt (Eq.4) --> output (no amp/trem/cab)
```

## Equations

The notation follows the paper. Bold **u** = (u^H, u^V) is the two-polarisation transverse deflection, x is the axial coordinate along the tine, t is time, and a subscript means a partial derivative. The paper gives no units; the SI units below are inferred.

**Eq. 1 (Hammer force, §5.2; after Avanzini & Rocchesso [14], Stulov [22], Hunt & Crossley [33])**

$$
F([\mathbf{x}],t)=\begin{cases}k\,\mathbf{x}(t)^{\alpha}+\lambda\,\mathbf{x}(t)^{\alpha}\,x_t(t) & \text{if } \sum_{xL}\mathbf{x}>0\\ 0 & \text{for } \sum_{xL}\mathbf{x}\le 0\end{cases}
$$

- **x** is the hammer-felt compression (m). It is the hammer position minus the beam deflection, distributed over the contact region. Σ_xL is a weighted sum over the contact area L.
- k is the contact stiffness (N/m^α).
- α is the nonlinearity exponent, which depends on the contact geometry (dimensionless; typical felt values are 2–3.5 in the literature, but this paper gives no value).
- λ is the material damping (N·s/m^(α+1)).
- x_t is the compression velocity (m/s).
- The hammer equation of motion is not given. Assume the standard form m_h·ẍ_h = −F (ESTIMATE of intent, per [14]).

**Eq. 2 (Rhodes tine PDE, §5.3; after Han, Benaroya & Wei [28])**

$$
\rho\,\mathbf{u}_{tt}+[EI\,\mathbf{u}_{xx}]_{xx}-\tfrac{1}{2}EA\,\mathbf{u}_{xx}\cdot K(\mathbf{u})-\kappa\,\mathbf{u}_{2x2t}-F(\mathbf{u}^V[x],t)=0
$$

- ρ = ρ(x) is the mass per unit length, which is non-homogeneous and includes the tuning-spring mass (kg/m). The paper writes ρ, but the dimensionally correct form is ρA.
- E is Young's modulus (Pa).
- I: the paper calls it the "radius of gyration". Dimensionally it must be the area moment of inertia, I = πd⁴/64 for a round rod (m⁴). This is a likely mistake in the paper.
- A is the cross-sectional area (m²).
- K(**u**) is the Kirchhoff-like tension integral (Eq. 3).
- κ is the coefficient of the mixed term u_{xxtt} (written u_{2x2t}). This is the shear/rotary-inertia correction of the shear-beam model; its value is not given.
- F is the hammer force (Eq. 1), applied to the V polarisation only.
- The paper says the rotation angle is not considered ("not considering the angle of rotation").
- Boundary conditions for the Rhodes beam are not stated. Presumably clamped at x = 0 and free at x = l (cantilever).

**Eq. 3 (Kirchhoff coupling)**

$$
K(\mathbf{u})=\int_0^{l}\left[(u^H_x)^2+(u^V_x)^2\right]dx
$$

- l is the tine length (m). The integral is dimensionless.
- This is the only coupling path between the H and V polarisations (Figs. 4 and 11 show the resulting non-planar motion).

**Eq. 4–5 (Faraday's law, §5.4)**

$$
\epsilon=-\frac{\partial\Psi_B}{\partial t},\qquad \Psi_B=\int \vec B\cdot d\vec S
$$

- ε is the EMF (V), Ψ_B is the flux (Wb) and S is the coil cross-section surface.
- Back-EMF and induced-current effects are explicitly ignored.

**Eq. 6 (magnetic point charge)**

$$
B_z=B_0\frac{\Delta z}{|r_{21}|^3}
$$

- B_0 is the charge strength.
- Δz is the z-offset between source and field point.
- r_21 is the vector from the source point to the field point.

**Eq. 7 (field in front of the steel tip; 3 segments of the pole-piece profile, Fig. 6)**

$$
B_z(x',z')=|B_{tine}|\left[\int_a^b\frac{\sigma(z'-z(x))x}{[(x'-x)^2+(z'-z(x))^2]^{3/2}}dx+\int_b^c\frac{\sigma(z'-z_k)x}{[(x'-x)^2+(z'-z_k)^2]^{3/2}}dx+\int_c^d\frac{\sigma(z'-z(x))x}{[(x'-x)^2+(z'-z(x))^2]^{3/2}}dx\right]
$$

- σ is the constant magnetic charge density.
- z(x) is the pole-tip surface profile on the sloped flanks [a, b] and [c, d]. z_k is the constant z of the flat top [b, c]. a_x…d_x and a_y…d_y are the breakpoints in the x- and y-planes (Fig. 6, values not given).
- The extra factor "x" in the numerators is how the paper prints it. It is probably a typo for the integration variable or a weighting; treat it with suspicion.
- The (y', z') plane is computed the same way.

**Eq. 8 (tip trajectory)**

$$
z'=r-\sqrt{r^2-(x')^2},\qquad x'=\hat{x}\,\sin(2\pi f_{tine}t)
$$

- r is the radius of the arc, equal to the effective tine length from the fixation point (m).
- x̂ is the tip amplitude (m) and f_tine is the fundamental frequency (Hz).
- In the paper this sinusoid is used to *derive and visualise* the pickup weighting (Fig. 7). In the full model, x' presumably comes from the beam tip deflection instead.

Implied Rhodes output (synthesis of Eqs. 4–8; the paper does not print this exact form):

$$
v_{out}(t)\propto-\frac{d}{dt}\Phi\big(x'(t),y'(t)\big)=-\Big(\partial_{x'}\Phi\,\dot x'+\partial_{y'}\Phi\,\dot y'\Big)
$$

**Eq. 9 (Wurlitzer reed, §5.5)**

$$
\rho u_{tt}+[EIu_{xx}]_{xx}-\kappa u_{2x2t}-f(x,t)=0,\qquad u|_0=0,\quad k' G A u_x|_L=0
$$

- k' is the shear coefficient and G is the shear modulus.
- The reed has 1 polarisation and no Kirchhoff term.

**Eq. 10–12 (Wurlitzer capacitive pickup, §5.6)**

$$
i(t)=C(t)\frac{\partial u(t)}{\partial t}+u(t)\frac{\partial C(t)}{\partial t}\;\rightarrow\; i(t)=u_0\frac{\partial C(t)}{\partial t},\qquad C_i=\frac{Q_i}{U_i},\quad Q_i=\epsilon_t\oint_A\vec E\cdot d\vec A
$$

- The simplification to i = u0·dC/dt assumes a constant supply voltage and linear charging.

**Eq. 13–15 (hardware FD operators, §6.4)**

$$
\hat\delta_x=T_\Delta[\hat\epsilon_{\Delta x+},-\hat\epsilon_{\Delta x-}],\qquad \hat\delta_{xx}=T_\Delta[(\hat\epsilon_{\Delta x-}),(<1),(\hat\epsilon_{\Delta x+})],\qquad \hat\delta_{4x}=\hat\delta_{xx}*\hat\delta_{xx}
$$

- ε̂ is a register read of the neighbouring cell.
- (<1) is a left shift, i.e. ×2 in two's-complement fixed point.
- T_Δ = 1/Δx. The material parameters are folded into a single multiplicand per operator at initialisation.

**Eq. 16 (symplectic-Euler split of Eq. 2, §6.5)**

$$
\hat\delta_t v=[-\hat\delta_{xx}+\hat\delta_{tt}]\,\hat\delta^{M}_{xx}\mathbf{u}+K(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},t)+F([\mathbf{x}],t),\qquad \hat\delta_t u=v
$$

- v = u_t. The superscript M marks a δ_xx that carries the material/mass weighting (variable stiffness and mass).
- This is the paper's notation exactly as printed. Read literally, it is −δ_xx(M δ_xx u) (bending) plus a δ_tt δ_xx u term (the κ shear/rotary correction).
- Symplectic Euler means v is updated first, then u uses the new v: u^{n+1} = u^n + k·v^{n+1}.

## Discretization / algorithm

- **Scheme:** explicit FD in space with centred difference operators (Eqs. 13–15), and symplectic (semi-implicit) Euler in time (Eq. 16). The paper sends details to [21], [20].
- **Arithmetic:** signed two's-complement fixed point on the FPGA (§6.4). Multiplication by 2 is replaced by a shift. Material and geometry coefficients are pre-multiplied into one constant per operator at initialisation. This is directly relevant to the FM-1.
- **Sample rate / time step:** not stated for the model. The measurements were at 44.1 kHz. The FPGA FD clock is not given.
- **Grid size / state:** not stated. ESTIMATE of the state: 2 polarisations × N nodes × (u, v) = 4N words, plus hammer state (position and velocity). For N = 20–40, that is 80–160 state words per voice.
- **Parallelism:** all nodes are updated concurrently on the FPGA. The sequential dependency is only v then u (§6.3).
- **Stability:** no condition is given in the paper. External reference (Bilbao [29], not from this paper): for an explicit stiff-beam scheme, μ = κ_b·k/h² ≤ 1/2, with κ_b = sqrt(EI/(ρA)), k = 1/fs and h = Δx. The κ·u_xxtt shear term is implicit in time and needs a (tridiagonal) solve, or it must be handled explicitly via the δ_tt δ_xx form. The paper does not say which; this is an open question. The Kirchhoff term is nonlinear and explicit, so it may need a small step or energy-conserving treatment at large amplitudes. The paper writes no damping term, so the loss model is unknown.
- **Operation count per sample per voice (ESTIMATE, from Table 1):**
  - Per node per polarisation: δ4x (1 mult, 5 add, 4 shift) + mixed term δtt·δxx (≈2 mult, 4 add) + Kirchhoff term u_xx·K (δxx: 1 mult, 2 add, +1 mult) + v/u update (2 mult-add) ≈ **6–7 mult, ~13 add**.
  - Kirchhoff integral: N squared differences per polarisation, so 2N mult + 2N add, once per sample.
  - Total ≈ 2·N·(7 + 1) ≈ **16N mult**. With N = 30, that is **≈ 500 MAC + ~800 add/sub per sample per voice**.
  - Pickup: 2-D LUT lookup with interpolation plus a differentiator, ≈ 10–20 ops.
  - Hammer (only during contact, a few ms): pow(x, α) needs a LUT, ≈ 10 ops.
- **8 voices at 44.1 kHz:** ≈ 8 × 44 100 × ~1300 ≈ **4.6·10⁸ ops/s** (ESTIMATE). This is far beyond the FM-1 budget (see below).

## Measured / reference behaviour

Use these as validation targets:

- **Tine motion is nearly sinusoidal; the pickup output is not** (Fig. 3). The direct-out voltage shows two sharp, asymmetric spikes per tine cycle, with an alternating large/small pattern and a sawtooth-like edge. This shape is caused entirely by the nonlinear pickup field weighting plus d/dt (Fig. 7).
  - Validation target: drive the pickup model with a pure sinusoid at about the measured amplitude and offset. You should get the 2-spike-per-period, alternating-amplitude waveform.
- **Rest-position offset controls timbre** (Fig. 7a vs 7b).
  - Tine centred on the pole (Fig. 7a): near-symmetric weighting, so the output has mostly odd/second-order distortion and looks smooth.
  - Displaced mid-point (Fig. 7b): asymmetric weighting and a strongly asymmetric, spiky output. The paper says 7b is "close to a realistic playing condition".
- **Two-polarisation motion** (Fig. 4, measured).
  - Vertical (hammer direction) dominates, at about 4–7× the horizontal amplitude (ESTIMATE from axis read-off).
  - The phase plot is a parallelogram/whirl shape, i.e. a precessing elliptical orbit.
  - The horizontal motion comes from nonlinear coupling or hammer-tip imperfections (§4.2).
  - The simulated model (Fig. 11) reproduces the shape qualitatively: horizontal grows from zero, peaks mid-note and decays (energy exchange).
- **Velocity to timbre** (Fig. 10): five notes with increasing hammer velocity. Higher velocity gives more and stronger upper partials (to about 3 kHz) and more broadband energy at the attack (vertical streaks at each onset). This is the "bark" mechanism (§7.2).
- **Attack:** a broadband transient at each onset in Fig. 10 (hammer contact). No contact durations are given.
- **Decay rates / T60:** not given.
- **Partial frequency ratios:** not given. Fig. 10 shows a harmonic-looking series, which is consistent with the harmonics coming from pickup nonlinearity rather than from inharmonic beam modes.
- **Wurlitzer** (Fig. 5): the reed motion is nearly sinusoidal, and the output voltage is spiky and asymmetric (capacitive dC/dt). Adding tip mass lowers f0 (Fig. 12).

## FM-1 implementation notes

Assessment for AC791N, no FPU, 44.1 kHz, 256-frame blocks, 8 voices, < 2.9 ms per block:

1. **The full FD model is not feasible.** The ESTIMATE is ~1300 ops/sample/voice with N ≈ 30. At 8 voices that is ~4.6·10⁸ ops/s, against a budget of roughly 0.5 × CPU clock (clock unverified; even at 320 MHz with single-cycle MAC, the 50% budget is ~1.6·10⁸ cycles/s). Load/store overhead on a scalar core makes it worse. The paper's model was written for a massively parallel FPGA. Do **not** port the beam FD.

2. **What to keep from this paper (the valuable part): the pickup model.**
   - Precompute on the host a 1-D (or 2-D for H/V) table Φ(x') of flux vs. tip displacement. Use Eq. 7 integrated over the pole profile, with the circular-arc correction z' = r − sqrt(r² − x'²) of Eq. 8 folded into the table. Alternatively, fit a smooth function such as a Lorentzian/Gaussian-derivative shape.
   - At runtime: `y = dΦ/dx(x) · ẋ`, using a second table dΦ/dx (to avoid a noisy differentiator) multiplied by the modal velocity, which you already have from the oscillator state. Alternatively, use `y = Φ(x[n]) − Φ(x[n−1])`.
   - Store Φ as Q15 with 256–1024 entries and linear interpolation: ~6 ops/sample.
   - The rest offset (Fig. 7b) and the effective field width should be per-key parameters, or at least global "voicing" parameters. They are the main timbre controls.

3. **Replace the beam with modal oscillators.**
   - V polarisation: 1–3 damped modes (fundamental plus maybe the first cantilever overtone, ratio ≈ 6.27 for a uniform cantilever; this is textbook, not from this paper; the tuning spring mass shifts it).
   - H polarisation: 1 mode, slightly detuned. Couple it weakly, either with a small linear cross-term or with an amplitude-dependent term ∝ (u_H² + u_V²) emulating the Kirchhoff K(u) of Eq. 3, to get the Fig. 4/11 whirl and beating.
   - Each mode is a 2-pole resonator (2 state words, ~3 MAC). Cost ≈ 15–25 MAC per voice-sample including the pickup, i.e. ~8 × 44 100 × 25 ≈ 9·10⁶ MAC/s. That is feasible.
   - Use a coupled-form or "magic circle" oscillator (`s += e*c; c -= e*s`). It is stable in fixed point and has no drift. Precompute e = 2·sin(π f/fs) and the per-sample decay factors in Q1.30 per key.

4. **Hammer:** the Hunt–Crossley model with x^α needs pow(). Options:
   - (a) Precompute on the host an excitation-force pulse shape per velocity layer (half-sine-like, with width and peak depending on v0) and store it as a short table (~64–256 samples).
   - (b) Run the contact ODE with an x^α LUT for the few ms of contact. This costs little because it is active only during contact.
   - Either way, velocity mainly needs to set the initial amplitude. The "bark" then comes for free from the pickup nonlinearity. That is the paper's central qualitative claim (Fig. 7, 10).

5. **Q-format concerns:**
   - Displacement in Q1.15/Q1.31 normalised to the pickup table range. Clamp the index to the table bounds, because large hammer hits drive x past the field window.
   - Decay factors close to 1 (T60 of several seconds at 44.1 kHz gives 1 − r ≈ 1e-5) need Q1.31 coefficients and a 32×32 to 64-bit multiply with shift (64-bit multiply is allowed; no 64-bit division is needed).
   - The derivative output scales with f0, so normalise per key with a precomputed gain table.
   - Kirchhoff-style coupling (x²) can overflow Q15; keep a 32-bit intermediate.

6. **Lookup tables to precompute on the host:**
   - per-key f0 → oscillator coefficients and decay factors
   - per-key pickup offset/width
   - Φ(x) and dΦ/dx(x), shared, possibly a few variants by register
   - velocity → hammer amplitude/pulse
   - optional H-mode detune and coupling coefficients

7. **Risks:**
   - The paper has no numeric parameters, so the pickup geometry (a–d, r, offset) must come from [18], [27] or a measurement/fit to recordings.
   - Its Eq. 7 has a probable typo.
   - Pickup aliasing: the nonlinear map plus d/dt produces harmonics up to the Nyquist frequency for high notes and hard hits. Consider 2× oversampling of just the pickup stage, or keep the table smooth (band-limited Φ). This costs ~2× the pickup ops only.
   - The tonebar, damper, release, amp and tremolo are not covered by this source and need other sources.

## Gaps & open questions

- **No numeric parameters at all:** material constants, geometry, hammer, pickup, grid, time step, damping. Check [18] (Springer 2017) and [27] (Falaize & Hélie 2017, which has parameter tables) for values.
- **No loss/damping term in Eq. 2/16.** How decay is produced is unspecified, and so are per-partial decay rates and damper behaviour.
- **Boundary conditions** for the Rhodes tine are not stated (Eq. 9 gives them only for the Wurlitzer).
- **Eq. 7** has an unexplained factor x in the numerator, and the pole-profile function z(x) and breakpoints are not given.

- **The tonebar (second prong of the tuning fork) is not modeled,** so its effect on sustain/beating is unknown from this source.
- **"Coupling ... as proposed in [?]"** is a broken citation in the paper (§5.3).
- **The cm-scale deflections in Figs. 4/11** may be uncalibrated or mislabelled units.
- **Only one tine was measured,** of unknown key (≈77 Hz ESTIMATE). There is no register dependence.
